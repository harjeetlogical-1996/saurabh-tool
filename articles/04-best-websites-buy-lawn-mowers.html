What to look for before buying lawn equipment online

Before you click buy, run through this quick checklist. It is the same one we used to score the stores above.

Outdoor power buying checklist

  • Gas vs battery: battery tools are quiet, low-maintenance, and great for small to medium yards; gas still wins for big lawns and heavy chainsaw work.
  • Yard size: under 1/4 acre suits a push or battery mower; 1/4 to 1/2 acre a self-propelled; over 1/2 acre a riding or zero-turn mower.
  • Battery platform: if you buy battery tools, stick to one brand (EGO, Greenworks, STIHL) so batteries are interchangeable.
  • Deck size: a wider mower deck cuts faster, but make sure it fits your gates and storage.
  • Warranty: 2 to 3 years is common; premium brands like Husqvarna and STIHL often offer longer.
  • Assembly & delivery: big mowers ship by freight; confirm whether assembly and liftgate delivery are included.

How much should you spend on a lawn mower?

Here is a realistic budget guide based on the live pricing we saw across these stores:

  • $150 to $350: push mowers and entry battery mowers (Hart, Murray) for small lawns.
  • $350 to $800: self-propelled gas and quality battery mowers (EGO, Toro) for medium yards.
  • $800 to $1,500: premium battery systems, robotic mowers, and small riding mowers.
  • $1,500 and up: riding and zero-turn mowers (Cub Cadet, Husqvarna) for large properties.

Red flags to avoid when buying lawn equipment online

Watch out for these

  • Battery tools sold without the battery or charger; check what is included.
  • No clear warranty, or a brand you cannot find any reviews for.
  • Riding mowers with no mention of freight, assembly, or liftgate delivery.
  • Prices far below the big retailers on a name brand; confirm it is new and authorized.
  • No parts or service support, which matters for gas engines and chainsaws.
