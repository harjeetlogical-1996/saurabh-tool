What to look for before buying a dirt bike online

Before you click buy, run through this quick checklist. It is the same one we used to score the stores above, and it will save you from an expensive mistake.

Buying checklist

  • Engine size vs rider: 50cc to 110cc suits kids and beginners, 125cc is the sweet spot for teens and lighter adults, and 250cc and up is for experienced riders. Do not overbuy on power.
  • Transmission: fully automatic (no clutch) is easiest to learn on; manual clutch gives more control once you progress.
  • Assembly: most budget bikes arrive 85 to 95 percent assembled and need the handlebars, front wheel, and fender fitted, plus oil and a tune. Budget an hour or a small shop fee.
  • Warranty: entry bikes often carry only 90 days; a longer frame warranty (like X-PRO's 1 year) is a real plus.
  • Shipping: check whether it is free, flat-rate, or LTL freight with a liftgate, and whether your address qualifies.
  • Real reviews: read recent Trustpilot and Google reviews for shipping damage and support response, not just the star average.

How much should you spend on a dirt bike?

Here is a realistic budget guide based on the live pricing we saw across these stores:

  • $500 to $900: kids' 50cc to 110cc bikes and basic pit bikes. Great for first-time and young riders.
  • $900 to $1,500: the popular 125cc class (Apollo X18, X-PRO X9, Coolster QG-214). Best all-round value for teens and beginner adults.
  • $1,500 to $2,500: larger 140cc to 250cc gas bikes and street-legal models for experienced riders.
  • $3,000 and up: premium electric dirt bikes like the E Ride Pro SR and Talaria, with serious speed and swappable batteries.

Red flags to avoid when buying a dirt bike online

Watch out for these

  • No physical address, phone number, or working support contact on the site.
  • Prices far below everyone else with no explanation; if it looks too cheap, it usually is.
  • No clear shipping, return, or warranty policy anywhere on the website.
  • Only glowing reviews on the site itself and nothing on Trustpilot, Google, or the BBB.
  • Pressure to pay by wire transfer, gift card, or crypto instead of a card or financing.
