What to look for in a solar generator

Solar generator buying checklist

  • Capacity (Wh): 500Wh runs phones and lights for a weekend; 1000Wh and up can run a fridge and essentials for hours.
  • Output (W): check the continuous watt output to be sure it runs your devices, not just the battery size.
  • Solar input: higher solar input watts mean faster recharging from panels; confirm panel compatibility.
  • Battery type: LiFePO4 batteries (EcoFlow, Anker, Jackery) last far longer than older lithium.
  • Warranty: 2 years is common; Anker SOLIX offers 5 years.
  • Panels included: a kit (4Patriots) bundles panels; buying separately can save money if you match specs.

How much should you spend on a solar generator?

  • $250 to $600: small units (Anker C300, Jackery Explorer 1000) for camping and short outages.
  • $600 to $1,200: mid-size systems (EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus, Bluetti AC180) that run a fridge and essentials.
  • $1,500 and up: large solar kits and emergency systems (4Patriots, Nature's Generator) for serious backup.
