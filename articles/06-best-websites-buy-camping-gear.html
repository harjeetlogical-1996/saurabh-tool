What to look for before buying camping gear online

Before you click buy, run through this quick checklist. It is the same one we used to score the stores above.

Camping gear buying checklist

  • Tent capacity: buy one size up from your group; a "4-person" tent is comfortable for 2 to 3 with gear.
  • Season rating: 3-season gear covers most trips; 4-season is for winter and high wind.
  • Weight: car camping can be heavy and comfortable; backpacking needs light, packable gear.
  • Return policy: sizing and comfort matter. REI and CampSaver have generous, often free returns.
  • Rooftop tents: check your roof rack's dynamic and static weight limits before buying.
  • Recovery gear: match strap and shackle ratings to your vehicle's weight; Rhino USA lists clear ratings.

How much should you spend on camping gear?

Here is a realistic budget guide based on the live pricing we saw across these stores:

  • $100 to $300: a starter kit, budget tent, sleeping bag, and pad (Decathlon, CampSaver, Amazon).
  • $300 to $700: quality 3-season gear from REI Co-op and Backcountry that lasts for years.
  • $1,000 to $1,500: an entry overlanding setup with an OVS rooftop tent and basic recovery gear.
  • $3,000 and up: premium rooftop tents and complete overland systems (Rhino Adventure Gear).

Red flags to avoid when buying camping gear online

Watch out for these

  • No clear return policy; sizing and comfort issues are common with gear.
  • Rooftop tents sold without weight specs or roof-rack requirements.
  • Recovery straps and shackles with no stated working load rating.
  • Unknown brands far cheaper than REI or OVS with no reviews.
  • Slow shipping that will not arrive before your trip; check delivery estimates.
