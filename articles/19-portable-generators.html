What to look for in a portable generator

Portable generator buying checklist

  • Running vs starting watts: running watts is continuous power; starting watts is the brief surge appliances need. Match starting watts to your biggest appliance.
  • Fuel type: dual-fuel (gas or propane) gives flexibility during shortages; inverters run cleaner and quieter.
  • Inverter vs conventional: inverters are quieter and safe for sensitive electronics; conventional units make more raw power for less money.
  • Noise: for camping or RVs, choose a quiet inverter like the Honda EU2200i.
  • Runtime: check hours per tank at half load, and whether it has an electric start.
  • Warranty: 2 to 3 years is common; buy from the brand or authorized seller for coverage.

How much should you spend on a portable generator?

  • $300 to $600: value gas units (Westinghouse WGen3600v) for basic backup.
  • $600 to $1,000: dual-fuel and mid-size units (Champion 4375W, Generac GP) for outages and RVs.
  • $1,000 to $1,300: premium quiet inverters (Honda EU2200i) for camping and sensitive electronics.
  • $1,200 and up: high-wattage dual-fuel units (DuroMax XP13000HX) for job sites and whole-home backup.
