What to look for in a cheap e-bike

Saving money should not mean buying junk. Run through this checklist before you buy a budget e-bike.

Cheap e-bike buying checklist

  • Battery warranty: the battery is the priciest part to replace, so a clear 1 to 2 year warranty matters most on a cheap bike.
  • Watt-hours (Wh): ignore vague mile claims and check the Wh. Around 500Wh gives genuine daily range.
  • UL certification: look for UL-certified batteries for fire safety, especially on the cheapest bikes.
  • Motor: 500W handles flat city riding; 750W is better for hills and heavier riders.
  • Refurbished option: a certified refurbished premium bike (Upway) often beats a cheap new one for the same money.
  • Support: confirm there is US-based support and parts availability before you buy.

How much should you spend on a cheap e-bike?

  • $500 to $800: entry folding and fat-tire bikes (Heybike, ENGWE, Amazon). Fine for casual, short rides.
  • $800 to $1,000: the budget sweet spot, the Lectric XP and similar offer real quality and support.
  • Refurbished: $700 to $1,200 on Upway gets you a $2,000-class premium bike with a warranty.
