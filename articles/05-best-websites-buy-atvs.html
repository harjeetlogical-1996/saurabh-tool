What to look for before buying an ATV or UTV online

Before you commit, run through this quick checklist. It is the same one we used to score the sites above.

ATV and UTV buying checklist

  • Use case: sport quads and RZRs are built for speed and trails; utility ATVs and RANGER-style UTVs are for work, hauling, and hunting.
  • New vs used: new gets you warranty and the latest tech; used (eBay, Autotrader, ATV Trader) saves money but demands a careful inspection.
  • Engine size: 90cc to 110cc for youth, 450cc to 700cc for most adults, 850cc and up for performance and heavy work.
  • Seating: choose a 2-seat or 4-seat UTV based on how many riders you carry.
  • Financing: RideNow and Polaris offer strong financing; check the total cost, not just the monthly payment.
  • Delivery: big machines ship via dealer or freight. Confirm delivery, setup, and any dealer prep fees.

How much should you spend on an ATV or UTV?

Here is a realistic budget guide based on the live pricing we saw across these sites:

  • $700 to $3,000: youth quads and budget Chinese ATVs (Tribal, MotoBuys) for beginners and kids.
  • $5,000 to $10,000: used name-brand ATVs and entry side-by-sides (eBay, Autotrader, Tracker).
  • $10,000 to $20,000: new utility and recreation UTVs like the Honda Pioneer 700 and Yamaha Wolverine.
  • $20,000 and up: performance side-by-sides like the Polaris RZR and Kawasaki Teryx KRX 1000.

Red flags to avoid when buying an ATV or UTV online

Watch out for these

  • Private sellers who refuse a VIN, service history, or an in-person inspection.
  • Deals far below market with pressure to wire money or pay before seeing the machine.
  • No title or unclear ownership, a major problem for registration.
  • Unknown ATV brands far cheaper than the rest with no parts or warranty support.
  • Dealer listings that hide prep, freight, or documentation fees until checkout.
