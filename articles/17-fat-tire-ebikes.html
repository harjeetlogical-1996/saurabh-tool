What to look for in a fat-tire e-bike

Fat-tire e-bike buying checklist

  • Motor power: 750W is the sweet spot for fat tires, which need more power to roll than skinny tires.
  • Tire width: 4-inch tires are the standard for sand and snow; wider means more float and grip.
  • Battery (Wh): fat tires draw more power, so aim for 500Wh or more for real range.
  • Suspension: a front suspension fork (Aventure 3) greatly improves comfort on rough terrain.
  • Weight: fat-tire bikes are heavy (60 to 75 lbs); folding models help with storage and transport.
  • Warranty: 2 years (Aventon, Velotric) is best, especially on the battery.

How much should you spend on a fat-tire e-bike?

  • $700 to $1,200: budget folding fat-tire bikes (Heybike, ENGWE, Lectric XP).
  • $1,200 to $1,800: quality all-terrain bikes (Rad RadRover, Velotric Nomad).
  • $1,800 to $2,200: premium fat-tire bikes like the Aventon Aventure 3 with suspension and a 2-year warranty.
  • Refurbished: Upway gets you a premium fat-tire bike for closer to $1,000 with a warranty.
